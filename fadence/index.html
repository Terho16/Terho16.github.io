Fadence

Fadence is a sensory-driven wearable designed to intervene at the edge of awareness, before overthinking solidifies into sleeplessness. Inspired by the natural rhythms of forgetting, it listens to the body’s micro-signals and responds with gentle heat or vibration, forming a soft cadence that guides the user from alertness to rest. No screens. No commands. Only the fading rhythm of thought, restructured by the body.

01 · Problem

A mind that won’t slow down

Short-term insomnia often starts with racing thoughts. Most sleep aids still ask for your attention: a light to follow, a sound to listen to, or a headset to wear.

Yet beneath these layers lies one consistent thread:

The mind’s refusal to rest.

Concept Sketches

03 · Form

Shaped to rest on the neck and back, with no straps

Inspired by the soft grip of an octopus and the structural elegance of insect shells, the device wraps the body like a second skin: seamless, strapless, and intuitive. Its segmental form offers protection without pressure, intimacy without intrusion.

System Structures

Arduino NanoCore controller that receives and processes biometric data in real time
LaptopFor data visualization, testing, and threshold calibration
PPG SensorContinuously tracks HRV to detect signs of cognitive arousal
Vibration Motor ModuleSends micro-pulses to interrupt mental rumination before sleep
Graphene Heating PatchApplies subtle warmth to induce calm and downregulate arousal
  • Sleep-friendlyNo gels or headgear, suitable for passive nighttime monitoring.
  • Detects overthinkingHRV changes signal cognitive arousal before full wakefulness.
  • Compact integrationSensor and intervention share the same area, the neck or ear, minimizing intrusion.

A minimal yet complete system: each module plays a precise role, sensing, deciding, and intervening, to disrupt the cognitive buildup behind short-term insomnia.

Key decision

Listen quietly, then answer in two gentle steps

A PPG sensor near the neck, the same kind smartwatches use, tracks heart-rate variability (HRV). When HRV suggests the mind is still busy, Fadence first warms up. If that doesn’t help, a soft vibration pulse follows. When HRV settles, it fades out.

Why the neck: blood flow is stable there, the artery is close to the skin, and the sensor stays out of the way during sleep.
